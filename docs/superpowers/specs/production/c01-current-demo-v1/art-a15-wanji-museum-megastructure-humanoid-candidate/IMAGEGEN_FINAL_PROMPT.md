## ImageGen prompt

Create one wide 3:2 professional Japanese super-robot production concept sheet showing ONE coherent transforming megastructure design, the WANJI Museum, with two principal forms and a small transformation-mapping strip between them. The first and strongest read MUST be: **a vast enterable high-tech museum of robot culture, an old city convention center whose whole public architecture can lift off as a slow low-altitude mobile exhibition fortress**. It must NOT read as any kind of boat, yacht, speedboat, cruise liner, naval ship, spacecraft, or vehicle with a building placed on top.

COMPOSITION AND VISUAL HIERARCHY:
- Left 48%: hero three-quarter view of the C01 low-altitude touring form, hovering slowly just above a broken urban foundation. Camera is at low pedestrian/city-block height so the object feels 130–160 meters long and 35–45 meters high. This is visually a ROBOTICS MUSEUM MEGASTRUCTURE FIRST, mobile fortress second.
- Center 17%: 3 clean structural transformation silhouettes / exploded relationship diagrams, with no decorative text. Show architecture folding by huge joints, rails and load paths, not magical morphing.
- Right 35%: full-body future humanoid giant robot direction preview, standing solidly and front-three-quarter, clearly made from the exact same museum architecture. It must feel like a unique museum-city super robot, not a generic industrial robot and not a speedboat Transformer.
- Small lower-left cutaway inset of the fixed-site state: the above-ground public museum and an equally large or larger buried B1+B2 megastructure under the old convention center. Show municipal pipes and later-added glass vestibules disconnecting and remaining on the ground.

THE PUBLIC MUSEUM MUST DOMINATE THE FIRST READ:
- A monumental 70 x 45 meter clear-span central exhibition hall, 24 meter clear height, expressed outside through a huge old high-tech space-frame / sawtooth roof and broad structural bays.
- Two multi-level public gallery wings visibly wrapping the main hall, with repeated human-scale windows, elevated walkways, exhibition lighting and layered public circulation.
- A generous monumental public entrance hall that doubles as the old public navigation console; it must look like an entrance to a beloved robot museum, never a cockpit or ship bridge.
- Several enormous full-scale mecha display windows / service apertures where the silhouette of one half-kneeling 18 meter Guardian Z can be seen as a clear scale anchor. Keep the museum itself vastly larger than that robot.
- The above-ground museum is not a tiny cap. Its hall roof, gallery wings, entrance and public windows form the principal silhouette and at least half the visible height and identity.
- Use high-tech museum architecture: exposed deep trusses, long-span columns, suspended exhibit gantries, old illuminated exhibition bands, civic wayfinding shapes, patched translucent panels, large public stair / ramp volumes. It should feel like a lived-in culture institution that has hosted generations of visitors.

UNDERGROUND / LOWER MEGASTRUCTURE:
- Beneath the public museum, reveal B1 and B2 with combined volume equal to or greater than the above-ground museum: a deep central load-bearing hangar spine, broad left/right structural masses, giant segmented lift and counterweight frames, one scarce heavy service bay, several modest medium berths, and a very deep black-box / resonance body.
- Do not make this a thin vehicle chassis or a smooth hull. It is the buried structural body of the old convention center: thick, stepped, orthogonal megastructure masses sharing the same columns and truss grid as the museum above.
- Give it weight through exposed colossal support columns, large scarred lifting collars, old annular propulsion rings / balancing rotors, asymmetric later expansions, repair plates, deep shadowed cavities, and severed city utility interfaces. Five or fewer large visual masses; avoid dense tiny greebles.
- The hovering touring form should sag slightly and show old settling asymmetry, as if automated balance is laboriously correcting it.

FORWARD DIRECTION — ABSOLUTELY NO BOAT PROW:
- The forward side is the SOUTH ROBOT GATE: a broad, tall, rectangular exhibition/service portal integrated into the museum facade.
- In front of that gate is a blunt, wide, rectangular external berth / civic forecourt that becomes the forward working deck when airborne. An 18 meter Guardian Z stands on it.
- This gate + plaza establishes forward direction like a huge convention center loading facade. There is NO pointed nose, NO tapered prow, NO curved white windshield, NO bridge tower, NO railings or luxury deck, NO sheer line, and NO continuous boat hull.
- The forward edge must remain broad, vertical, architectural and slightly asymmetric, closer to a giant public building threshold than any ship bow.

LOW-ALTITUDE TOURING FORTRESS CHARACTER:
- It moves like a city block reluctantly lifting, not like a fast craft. Show old rotor rings and balancing machinery underneath and at the rear sides, but no giant weapons.
- Limited tiny point-defense details may exist but must be visually subordinate to exhibition halls, public windows, support structure and civic architecture.
- Palette: warm aged ivory concrete / ceramic panels, faded museum vermilion, oxidized teal structural steel, dark charcoal B1/B2 machinery, warm amber exhibit windows, restrained cyan navigation light. Avoid glossy all-white yacht surfaces.
- Lighting: brighter readable stylized animation concept art, late-afternoon neutral sky, enough contrast to see the architecture and scale; not a dark military painting.

FUTURE HUMANOID TRANSFORMATION PREVIEW:
- The exact public central hall becomes the armored chest / upper torso volume; its huge exhibition aperture and warm-lit interior remain identifiable.
- The two gallery wings rotate and lock into broad shoulders and a layered architectural back, retaining public windows, walkways and roof trusses as recognizable museum structures.
- The public navigation hall and an observatory / civic beacon restructure into a clearly changed, heroic head with a distinct face and crown; it is not the original building sitting on top like a hat.
- The B1 load-bearing spine becomes the articulated torso spine; B2's deep left/right megastructure masses unfold into an enormous pelvis, two load-bearing legs and massive foundation-like feet. The lower body must visibly contain the stored volume seen in the cutaway.

- Humanoid proportions are colossal, architectural, stable and slightly old-fashioned: broad torso, huge bearing legs, civic-monument stance, distinct museum-city identity. It must NOT be a generic Gundam, modern military robot, skeletal industrial robot, or sleek Transformers-style sports vehicle robot.

STYLE AND CLARITY:
- High-end hand-painted production concept art for a Japanese strategy RPG / heroic robot animation, clear block design with 3–5 dominant masses, plausible transformation engineering, expressive but controlled detail, clean neutral presentation background.

- No readable generated text, no logos, no watermark, no frame captions.

HARD NEGATIVE CONSTRAINTS, REPEAT AS A DESIGN GATE:
NO yacht, NO speedboat, NO leisure craft, NO cruise ship, NO nautical hull, NO sharp prow, NO tapered nose, NO white curved cockpit windshield, NO luxury upper deck, NO naval aircraft carrier, NO battleship turrets, NO sleek spaceship, NO tank tracks, NO building glued onto a flat vehicle, NO three hanging pods, NO dangling cylinders, NO thin undercarriage, NO generic industrial humanoid. If the first form could plausibly be described as a ship or boat, the design has failed. It must first and unmistakably read as a high-tech ROBOT CULTURE MUSEUM MEGASTRUCTURE that can move.
