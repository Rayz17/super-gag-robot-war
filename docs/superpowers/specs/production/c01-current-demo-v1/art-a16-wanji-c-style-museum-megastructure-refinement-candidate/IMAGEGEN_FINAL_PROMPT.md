## 最终提示词

Use case: stylized-concept
Asset type: original game megastructure / transforming mobile museum production concept sheet

Primary request: Create ONE coherent, completely original redesign of the WANJI Museum using Image 1 only for approved structural content and Image 2 only for modern silhouette language. The result must preserve the approved “robot-culture museum mother body,” while replacing Image 1's boxy, Soundwave-like, generic industrial styling with a contemporary, elegant, low-horizontal, massive transforming-megastructure design. It should have the spectacular scale and legible transformation engineering of a classic Japanese transforming space-fortress animation, but must not copy any existing franchise silhouette, parts, color blocking, head, bridge, logos, or transformation sequence.

Composition:
- One wide 3:2 professional concept-art sheet on a pale neutral background, no border, no typography.
- Left 52%: the strongest hero view, a low city-block-height three-quarter view of the C01 touring state, slowly hovering just above a damaged urban foundation. The camera is near rooftop/pedestrian scale so the body unmistakably feels 130–160 meters long and 35–45 meters high.
- Center 15%: three small, clean, ghosted structural relationship stages showing only large hinges, sliding load paths, rotating gallery roots and the deep spine reorganizing; no magical morphing and no copied franchise transformation.
- Right 33%: a full-body future humanoid direction preview, standing in a stable heroic front-three-quarter pose and built from the exact same museum architecture. Its redesigned head, chest, shoulders, pelvis, legs and foundation feet must be clearly traceable to the touring form, yet read as a unique modern heroic giant rather than a generic industrial robot.
- Small lower-left fixed-site cutaway inset: the public museum above ground and an equally large or larger B1/B2 body below ground. Show pedestrians, one fire truck, surrounding mid-rise roofs and a tiny 18-meter Guardian Z silhouette only as scale anchors.

Touring-form silhouette and architecture:
- First read: an enterable high-tech ROBOT CULTURE MUSEUM MEGASTRUCTURE; second read: a slow mobile exhibition fortress. Never first-read as a boat, yacht, ship, carrier or ordinary building.
- Use Image 2's design grammar without copying it: three broad stepped roof volumes sweep backward in a controlled asymmetric rhythm; long public window bands and gallery terraces follow those planes; the south public forecourt is a broad asymmetric fan-shaped civic threshold.
- Make the body modern through long compound surfaces, precise negative spaces, restrained diagonal load lines, broad chamfered planes, flush deep joints and a few continuous luminous exhibition bands. The silhouette is sleek in line flow but thick, weight-bearing and city-scaled in mass.
- Keep only 3–5 dominant masses: the layered museum crown, two broad but integrated B1 side bodies, one deep central B1/B2 load spine, and a restrained rear balance/propulsion cluster. Avoid repetitive boxes or a pile of rectangular modules.
- The main public exhibition hall remains a monumental clear-span volume, with multi-level galleries, warm human-scale entrances, visible interior bridges and several full-scale mecha display windows. The public museum is not a tiny cap and is not swallowed by armor.
- Beneath it, B1/B2 must be equal or greater in volume: a deep load-bearing hangar spine, one scarce heavy service bay, several modest medium berths, black-box depth, colossal lift collars and buried counterweight/rotor frames. This lower body is thick and massive, not a thin flying slab.
- The south robot gate and broad public forecourt become a blunt integrated forward working threshold. Its forward edge is wide, architectural and slightly asymmetric, never a pointed prow. One 18-meter Guardian Z stands there as a tiny scale anchor.
- The hovering body moves like a city block reluctantly lifting. Use a few buried annular rotors, balance apertures and deep lift cavities; no giant weapons. Show old settlement asymmetry being corrected by the machinery.
- Recover Image 2's lighter, more modern visual flow, but avoid glossy luxury surfaces. Materials are warm ivory ceramic-composite museum planes, satin desaturated teal/blue structural metal, charcoal depth, restrained vermilion museum accents, old bronze load rings, warm amber public windows and sparse cyan navigation light.

Future humanoid direction:
- Deep structural reorganization, not a building worn as external armor. The central hall becomes a modern layered chest volume; its exhibit aperture and warm public interior remain recognizable but are integrated into heroic anatomy.
- Gallery wings rotate and interlock into broad, swept shoulders and a continuous architectural back. Their window bands and long truss lines remain visible as museum DNA, not loose square shoulder boxes.
- The public navigation hall and civic beacon reconfigure into a completely changed, handsome, distinctive heroic head. Use a clean contemporary face, layered crown/beacon fins and strong negative space; do not leave an old building or dome sitting on top.
- The B1 spine becomes the articulated torso; the huge B2 masses fully become pelvis, long load-bearing legs and vast foundation feet. The legs must prove where the underground volume went.
- Proportions: monumental and modern, broad upper body, controlled waist, powerful long legs, stable foundation feet, a clear iconic silhouette. It must feel like a unique museum-city super robot, not a box robot, construction robot, military Gundam-like unit, or vehicle Transformer.
- Preserve long flowing diagonals and stepped public-architecture motifs across head crown, chest, shoulders, shins and feet so the humanoid and touring form share one visual language.

Rendering and mood:
- High-end hand-painted Japanese strategy-RPG / heroic-robot production concept art over precise industrial design, clean readable edges, controlled detail density, strong silhouette, sophisticated contemporary material separation.
- Bright neutral late-afternoon light, clear scale and architecture; not gloomy military concept art, not toy photography, not photoreal live action.

Hard constraints:

- No yacht, speedboat, leisure craft, cruise liner, naval ship, aircraft carrier, battleship, nautical hull, sharp prow, tapered nose, curved cockpit windshield, luxury decks, bridge tower, runway, railings, giant cannon, missile farm, tracks, wheels or tank chassis.

Success gate: At thumbnail size the left form must read as a modern, low, horizontal, colossal robot museum that can move, never a boat or a stack of boxes; the right form must read as its handsome, unique, deeply reorganized future humanoid, never a generic industrial robot.
