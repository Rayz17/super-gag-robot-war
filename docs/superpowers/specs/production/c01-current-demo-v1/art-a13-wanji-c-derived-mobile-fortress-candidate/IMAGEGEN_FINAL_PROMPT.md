# Wanji C-derived mobile-fortress Candidate prompt

Use case: stylized-concept
Asset type: original game environment / mobile-base vehicle concept sheet, later composed into a Chinese user-confirmation board

Primary request: Create ONE coherent original design for Wanji Museum, shown as three coordinated views on one wide clean concept-art sheet. This is not three alternatives. It is the same structure in three readings: (1) fixed museum with a ground cutaway, (2) fully recovered low-altitude touring fortress in flight, and (3) a restrained structural-deployment study suggesting future reconfiguration without revealing a humanoid form.

Subject and structural logic:
- Start from a distinctive C-family upper silhouette: an asymmetrical sequence of three broad stepped museum roofs and a fan-shaped public forecourt, but substantially redesign everything below it.
- The recognizable above-ground public museum is only roughly ONE THIRD of the total vertical body. Below the city ground line, show at least TWO THIRDS of the volume as an enormous hidden original touring body: one deep central load-bearing keel/spine, two huge lateral service-and-hangar hulls, a large old circulation/flight-reactor chamber, heavy collection-support decks, and deep underbelly machinery.
- In the fixed-museum view, the upper public lobby, main hall, warm window bands, human-scale entrances and exhibition galleries remain legible at city scale. The buried structure must look like the museum's original body, not a newly attached platform. Municipal pipes, a light glass entry annex and later surface supports are visibly separate and can remain behind.
- In the flight view, the stepped museum becomes the inhabited civic crown of a truly massive mobile fortress. The hidden lateral hulls and central keel fully emerge and open into a powerful deep-bellied silhouette. The south forecourt becomes a broad external berth / foredeck, not a military runway. Show restrained low-altitude lift apertures and one large old propulsion ring integrated into the aft body.
- Give the craft a credible future reconfiguration grammar using a few large seams, hinge drums, rotating side-hull roots, sliding central spine sections and lock collars. These should imply three possible operational states: cruise, expanded working fortress, and an unspecified later complete form. Do NOT draw a humanoid robot, arms, legs, a face, or a finished transformation.
- The large structural masses must clearly support public halls, collections, a B1 hangar spine, a limited heavy-service berth for one 18-25m robot, several 2-12m service berths, and external lock berths. Do not depict an infinite hangar.

Design character:
- Original civilian robot-culture museum first, old industrial touring machine second, emergency mobile fortress third.
- A late-20th-century Japanese transforming mobile-fortress design logic may inspire the clear massing and traceable mechanical joints, but do not copy any existing franchise ship, silhouette, colors, emblems or transformation.
- Strong iconic silhouette built from 3-5 large masses, not dense greeble. Public museum window bands and large roof planes must remain readable from far away.
- Materials: aged painted steel, renovated concrete-composite roof shells, broad smoked-glass public windows, old exposed load-bearing rings, later repair patches.
- Palette: warm ivory museum roofs; weathered slate blue-gray and deep desaturated teal lower hull; muted rust-orange and old service-yellow accents; warm amber public windows. Bright neutral presentation, not gloomy.

Composition:
- Wide 3:2 landscape sheet, pale warm neutral background, no border and no typography.
- Left 38%: fixed museum, elevated front-right three-quarter cutaway. A crisp horizontal city ground line exposes the entire underground two-thirds. Include a few tiny city buildings, a fire truck, and people only as scale cues.
- Center 42%: largest hero view, elevated front-right three-quarter low-altitude touring fortress, same orientation and exact same upper museum identity. Show the full deep keel and twin lateral hulls, with an 18m humanoid museum guardian standing on the foredeck as a small scale silhouette.
- Right 20%: clean side/underside structural study of the SAME craft with only three large ghosted deployment positions for side hulls/spine/hinges; no completed alternate body.
- Leave clean negative space around each view for later programmatic labels. Use consistent perspective and design landmarks across all three views.

Rendering: high-end original anime game mechanical concept art, clean painterly 3D concept render over precise industrial design drawing, readable edges, controlled detail density, clear material separation, production-design quality.

Constraints:
- No words, letters, numbers, logos, watermark, UI or annotations in the generated art.
- No modern aircraft-carrier island or runway, no battleship bow, no naval gun turrets, no missile farm, no huge cannon, no tracks or wheels, no tank chassis.
- No small exhibition hall simply sitting on a thin flying slab. No shallow underside. No generic rectangular spaceship.
- No palace, castle, cathedral, skyscraper, human city block, or toy-like kitbash.
- No humanoid transformation, no visible robot face, no arms/legs, no anthropomorphic stance.
- No dense tiny panels, pipes or repeated greebles overwhelming the 3-5 main structural blocks.
- The public museum must remain recognizable and inhabited; the lower touring body must be visibly much larger than the surface museum.
