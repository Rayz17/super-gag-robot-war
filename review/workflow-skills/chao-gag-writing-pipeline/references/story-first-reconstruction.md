# Story-First Reconstruction Mode

Use this mode when an existing Chao GAG run is structurally complete but reads like a design memo, ethics report, contract digest, or route fixture instead of a continuous robot-anime/game story. It is mandatory for the V35.9 C01-C12 creative reconstruction and remains the default repair mode for later chapters that inherit those episodes.

This reference changes **creative order**, not canon authority. It does not delete slot contracts, source absorption, lifecycle, branch, asset, or graph requirements. It moves them behind the first readable dramatic construction so that bookkeeping verifies a story instead of generating one.

## Core Diagnosis

The failure pattern to prevent is:

1. load many required rows and gates;
2. bind every row before a scene exists;
3. write prose that explains those bindings;
4. call field completeness a story pass.

This produces repeated episodes in which an efficient unified solution works, overreaches, is publicly corrected, a machine proves personhood through refusal or damage, an enemy records the sample, and every victory immediately receives an ethical invoice. Even strong mecha spectacle, romance, comedy, mystery, and route divergence become state-change summaries.

Story-First Mode reverses that pressure. The first question is not "which rows must be absorbed?" but "what would these people and machines urgently do next, and why would a player want to be there?"

## Authority Layers

Before reconstruction, classify inputs into four layers:

1. **Hard constraints**: accepted canon facts, locked lifecycle states, route legality, physical limits, safety boundaries, and user-confirmed relationship or roster commitments. These may veto a draft.
2. **Locked dramatic assets**: accepted scenes, images, emotional objects, mecha actions, jokes, relationship events, and consequences that must survive unless the user explicitly releases them.
3. **Candidate fuel**: IP pools, quote and scene anchors, what-if material, 50-issue solutions, daily-life hooks, deep research, value axes, and optional branch payloads. These may inspire scenes but cannot dictate their shape.
4. **Bookkeeping**: manifests, source absorption rows, asset tables, graph edges, selector fixtures, settlement fields, and review receipts. These are filled after the creative core exists.

Do not present layers 3 or 4 to the first creative pass as a checklist. Give that pass only the previous clear state, hard constraints, locked assets, and a compact list of open dramatic needs.

## One-Episode Creative Order

The core pipeline still produces exactly one episode per invocation. Run these passes in order.

### Pass 1: Single-Run Causal Spine

Write a compact causal spine before loading optional material:

- What concrete condition from the previous episode makes this episode happen now?
- Who wants what by the end of today, for a personal reason rather than a theme word?
- Which embodied opponent, rival, disaster, institution, or loved one actively prevents it?
- What tempting shortcut could genuinely work?
- What changes direction in the middle?
- What irreversible action ends the episode?
- What reward, relief, wonder, intimacy, victory, terror, or laughter does the player actually receive?
- Why can the next episode no longer be the same story at a larger scale?

For a branch episode, select one representative single-playthrough path for the reader spine. Record alternate route deltas later. Never narrate mutually exclusive routes as if they both happened.

### Pass 2: Dramatic Fingerprint And Repetition Veto

Declare the episode fingerprint in ordinary language:

- genre and tone;
- central desire;
- opponent intention and visible body/voice;
- dominant play fantasy;
- relationship pressure;
- one macro mystery movement;
- climax image;
- aftermath temperature;
- solution grammar.

Compare it with the previous three episodes and the current chapter. If the solution again depends on refusal, public disclosure, permission withdrawal, deliberate incompleteness, or accepting damage, rebuild before drafting unless repetition is an intentional reversal with a new emotional and gameplay result.

### Pass 3: Camera And Scene Chain

Write the readable scene chain before the production split. A thick episode normally needs enough scenes to show setup, desire, escalation, playable pressure, reversal, climax, and aftermath; do not satisfy this by padding summaries.

For every major scene, show:

- where bodies and machines are in space;
- what changes on screen and in sound;
- what a character attempts rather than what the theme means;
- what the mecha wants to accomplish positively;
- what surprises or delights the player;
- what remains unresolved when the camera leaves.

PV-scale events must include approach, transformation or impact process, reaction, and aftermath. Do not replace giant motion with "the unit completed the action" or immediately cut from a spectacle to an administrative settlement.

### Pass 4: Player-Action And Production Pass

Now identify the playable form:

- ADV/exploration/daily setup;
- controlled units and player objective;
- map or spatial problem;
- failure pressure;
- mid-encounter rule change;
- in-battle character event;
- branch or hidden condition earned through play;
- post-battle state.

The battle must carry story causality. "A battle happens" and "characters debate afterward" is not enough. A route cannot be only a menu ideology choice.

### Pass 5: Human Temperature Pass

Add or strengthen only the human-temperature material the episode needs:

- attraction, jealousy, embarrassment, tenderness, rupture, sacrifice, or comic sexual tension among adult/adult-coded characters;
- ordinary food, sleep, repair, clothing, hobbies, bad jokes, arguments, and private habits;
- a complete emotional release or uncomplicated pleasure when the arc needs one;
- ensemble reactions that do not wait for Jicun, Qihai, or Baiyuan to authorize personhood.

Do not immediately turn every ordinary object into testimony, debt, evidence, a world key, or an enemy sample. Do not require every episode to contain equal burn, tear, gag, romance, and tragedy. Give each episode an intentional energy function within the chapter curve.

### Pass 6: Source Selection And Contract Binding

Only after the readable core works:

1. select the minimum useful candidate rows;
2. build the Episode Input Manifest;
3. bind selected sources to existing scenes;
4. fill production split and state deltas;
5. resolve V35 slot, route, unit, asset, lifecycle, and skipped-world contracts;
6. record source absorption and graph/canon effects.

If a required row cannot bind without making a scene worse, classify it:

- hard conflict: revise the scene;
- stale candidate loadout: file an upstream migration request;
- optional fuel: skip it with reason;
- bookkeeping-only: keep it out of reader prose.

Never invent a weak scene merely to consume a row.

### Pass 7: Independent Review And Patch

Review the reader layer before showing reviewers engineering fields. The standing review surfaces are independent:

- showrunner/continuity;
- mecha/IP/spectacle;
- gameplay/production;
- character/relationship/emotion;
- canon/macro mystery/physical budget.

Reviewers must cite concrete scenes and return blockers or patch actions. Praise is not evidence. The orchestrator owns final integration and may not turn five reviews into a vote.

Patch locally when the failure is local. If the dramatic engine is wrong, rebuild the causal spine instead of adding fields or decorative paragraphs.

## Reader Assembly Rules

A reader assembly simulates one real playthrough.

- Choose one representative route pack and state the choice once.
- Put alternate routes in a separate delta appendix or separate route assembly.
- Do not include ids, fixtures, gate language, historical PASS claims, source rows, or production fields.
- Preserve episode boundaries, chapter escalation, and aftermath.
- Reader prose may foreground only the state changes the audience can feel in that scene.
- A reader should be able to retell what happened without using terms such as permission, testimony, route debt, source absorption, or state delta unless those terms are literally the visible conflict.

## Chapter And Prefix Reconstruction

Before rewriting an episode, create a chapter reconstruction map:

- chapter desire and external objective;
- distinct opponent intention;
- episode-by-episode energy wave;
- recurring core mecha and guest mecha roles;
- protagonist and relationship escalation;
- mystery answer and new question;
- physical/resource budget movement;
- representative route and alternate deltas;
- locked highlights;
- repeated solution grammar to avoid.

After every rebuilt chapter:

1. assemble the representative route;
2. read C01 through the current chapter without engineering fields;
3. obtain independent review receipts;
4. find the earliest root cause;
5. revise that chapter and replay forward;
6. cap the same root cause at three genuine rounds;
7. ledger residual risk and keep the strongest version after round three.

Do not advance because structural validators are green. Do not block forever because an optional improvement remains.

## Creative Acceptance Gate

An episode or chapter fails even when every contract field is complete if any blocker below is true:

- a reader cannot explain the concrete desire, opponent action, reversal, and result;
- the episode repeats the recent solution grammar without transformation;
- spectacle exists only as a result sentence or concept;
- major mecha act only as examples, refusals, malfunctions, or sacrificial props;
- the protagonist only suffers, approves, records, explains, or relinquishes;
- relationship material is an isolated anchor that does not change choice or consequence;
- the player cannot tell what is played;
- a branch is visible only in a menu or all branches collapse into the same reader prose;
- macro mystery appears only as terminology, logs, or a final hook;
- every victory is immediately cancelled by guilt, debt, or permanent damage;
- physical damage, energy, personnel, hangar space, or route consequences disappear between episodes;
- the prose requires engineering documentation to be emotionally legible.

A pass needs affirmative evidence, not absence of errors:

- a distinct episode fingerprint;
- at least one memorable process-based scene;
- a positive mecha desire expressed through action;
- an active protagonist or ensemble choice that changes capacity or limitation;
- a clear play experience;
- an intentional emotional temperature and release function;
- a visible macro or antagonist movement;
- a clean next-state handoff.

These are qualitative proofs, not quotas. One excellent scene may satisfy several surfaces; five labeled fields without a scene satisfy none.

## C13 Seam Test

C01-C12 reconstruction is not complete until C13 can start from the rebuilt state without reverting to the old summary grammar.

The C13 inheritance pack must contain:

- representative-route world, fleet, roster, relationship, mystery, physical, resource, and branch state;
- unresolved issues and three-round history;
- consumed and available IP/quote/what-if material;
- recurring opponents and their learned tactics;
- each core mecha's positive desire and next change window;
- protagonist capacities, wounds, authority, and relationship boundaries;
- a one-episode story-first brief for C13.

Draft one C13 pressure test episode through this mode. It passes only if readers can feel the inherited history without a recap dump and if its dramatic fingerprint differs from C12.
