# Chapter Preproduction and Human-Voice Reference

## Purpose

Use this reference between a chapter engine and a complete chapter outline or Episode Reader. It exists because a chapter engine can explain why a story happens while leaving blank:

- what entities actually exist;
- who can control what;
- where people and giant machines are;
- how a new ability reaches physical reality;
- which actions can be understood in a game camera;
- which assets are worth creating;
- how characters perform before they speak.

When those blanks are filled inside prose, the draft tends to invent device names, permissions, rooms, micro-actions, and explanatory dialogue. Preproduction moves that creativity into a small design space where ideas can be compared, simplified, rejected, and visualized before they become story facts.

## Non-Gold-Standard Rule

No accepted, user-confirmed, Working, or previously successful draft is a correctness gold standard.

- Canon status determines authority.
- Acceptance records proven value and raises the threshold for destructive rewrite.
- A thickness reference protects content class and scene density.
- None of these disables independent review.

When reviewing accepted material, preserve highlights by function, identify actual loss before revising, and prefer targeted repair. Still report real control, spatial, gameplay, voice, and continuity defects.

## Production Order

```text
series structure and outgoing state
-> chapter engine and human desires
-> minimum context compile
-> chapter entity/space/action preproduction
-> director board and battle/gameplay preplan
-> complete chapter outline
-> independent first-read causality audit
-> Layer4 closeout and continuity
-> pure-story Episode Reader
-> pure-story chapter Reader assembly
-> cross-chapter risk scan, Reader causality audit and human-voice review
-> episode gameflow/performance decomposition
-> optional open-information cards
-> story/gameflow/information consistency audit
-> gameplay, mecha and production review
-> deterministic lint
-> chapter review
-> explicit user creative acceptance
-> state backfill and writeback
```

Director, physical and battle boards remain backstage feasibility evidence. Give the prose writer their resolved boundaries, not their UI fields, phase lists, variable names or micro-action chains. The controlling split is `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-08-07-chao-gag-story-reader-gameplay-separation-v2.md`.

The order is not a waterfall prison. A better idea may appear during drafting. Pause, update the earliest affected layer, recheck downstream assumptions, then continue. Do not silently create a new fact in prose.

## Minimum Context Compiler

Read only:

- current production status and authority manifest;
- previous chapter's actual outgoing state;
- current chapter engine and must-reserve future peaks;
- involved character, relationship, mecha, faction, ability, spatial, and branch rows;
- selected IP/story, gameplay, daily-life, and emotional material;
- relevant known failures and accepted-highlight functions.

Record what was intentionally not loaded. Full Canon, graph, roster, and all historical drafts are debugging sources, not default context.

## Chapter Preproduction Model

### Character State

For each driving character:

- current desire;
- limited knowledge and active misunderstanding;
- current ability, authority, injury, and position;
- refusal line;
- active move if the protagonist never arrives;
- chapter-entry and chapter-exit change.

This is not another biography.

### Mecha and Enemy State

For each key unit:

- pilot, machine soul, AI, weapons, remote layer, and physical control owner;
- scale, movement, battlefield job, and lifecycle;
- one or more signature whole-body actions;
- explicit limitation and damage;
- why this unit, rather than a generic substitute, must perform the beat;
- player-visible method for recognizing the enemy rule and counterplay.

Do not count props, model boxes, hands, frames, terminals, or support UI as playable mecha.

### Ability and Mechanism

For each new causal ability:

```text
input
-> processor or interpreting subject
-> physical executor
-> output in the world
-> limit and refusal point
-> cost
-> player-visible proof
```

A drawing, name, model, memory, signature, or data record does not move a body by itself. Name the simulator, engineer, driver, actuator, system, person, or machine that executes it.

For an override, emergency rule, remote command, or safety-mode change, also
record the complete responsibility chain:

```text
named signer or operator
-> visible control station
-> exact owned target
-> ordinary authority
-> boundary crossed or consent omitted
-> local operator's visible response
```

“The institution changed it”, “the system continued”, and “the commander knew”
are not interchangeable. If the change causes the chapter crisis, the Reader
must show who issued it and how it reached the affected machine.

### Space and Scale

Use the smallest useful map:

- audience-facing place names;
- top view and section/scale view when necessary;
- paths between human areas, 18-24m units, giant units, and the carrier;
- simultaneous-event feasibility;
- loading, turning, falling, reaching, launch, docking, and evacuation paths;
- spaces reusable by SRPG, exploration, ADV, or short animation.

Prefer “停机坪、生产井、观景廊、发射轨” over a fresh technical noun unless the new term has repeated gameplay or visual value.

Track personnel transitions with the same care as giant-unit movement. When a
later accident, rescue, command, or death depends on who is inside a cockpit,
pod, train, ship, or room, the readable story must show the last meaningful
boarding, exit, return, and restraint or lock state. A state table cannot bridge
a missing human movement in prose.

One physical system receives one player-facing name within a chapter. Distinct
systems that create different responsibility chains must look and sound
different. An external ground tractor and an onboard low-speed thruster cannot
both be called “辅助推移” when different people control them.

Every vehicle, carrier, pod, test body, and transported object that changes
position in a decisive scene also needs a short movement declaration:

```text
normal carriage or propulsion
-> current source of force
-> operator and control station
-> whether it can move independently
-> how it stops
-> what remains available after damage or disconnection
```

“It is on the runway” does not explain how a 170 m carrier moved there. A
detachable pod is not a flying craft unless propulsion, fuel, control, and
recovery have been established.

Apply available-tool conservation across scene boundaries. Every vehicle,
winch, crane, tug, weapon, access path, operator, and control seat that remains
powered, connected, reachable, and competent at the end of one scene is still
an available solution in the next scene. Before a crisis may require a riskier
method, enumerate the simpler surviving alternatives and visibly show why each
is now destroyed, depowered, disconnected, blocked, reassigned, too slow, or
outside its operating envelope. An author may not merely stop mentioning a
ground tractor in order to make an onboard short-slide necessary.

When a scene breaches, opens, cuts, or removes part of a vehicle, mark the
pressure boundary and flightworthiness. An exterior equipment cover may be
opened while the fixed pressure bulkhead remains intact; cutting a pressure
hull requires decompression, evacuation, and later repair consequences.

For every rescue climax, close the force path all the way back to a stable
support:

```text
catch or attach
-> support the combined mass
-> arrest fall or drift
-> return to a stable surface or recovery envelope
-> release load and secure survivors
```

“A giant catches the pod” is only the middle of a rescue. If the giant cannot
hover, fly, brace, or reach ground, another body, anchor, winch, rail, field, or
structure must complete the return.

### Large-Action Vocabulary

List screenshot-readable actions before joint detail:

- transform;
- combine;
- catch a falling craft;
- lift a bridge;
- tear open a ring;
- brace a carrier;
- block a fleet weapon;
- turn away from escape to save someone.

Micro-actions may support a large action but cannot carry the climax alone.

### Asset and Reuse Model

List:

- new and reused characters, units, enemies, locations, props, UI, Cut-ins, maps, and animations;
- what can be represented by existing art or a short map event;
- what exists only in author logic and should not become an asset;
- expected future reuse.

A one-use device receives no unique name or asset unless it changes a major image, player rule, causal choice, or future state.

## Load Tiers

### Light

Established cast, space, abilities, and scale. Fold preproduction into the director board and record a skip reason.

### Standard

One new place, ability, enemy rule, or control chain. Produce one readable model and optional machine table.

### Heavy

Required when any of these apply:

- new large environment;
- multiple mecha scales in one scene;
- carrier or mecha transformation;
- cockpit, machine-soul space, and physical battlefield cross;
- first use of a new ability;
- three or more factions in active combat;
- victory depends on spatial structure.

Add a top-view graybox, scale/control diagram, map graybox, and four-to-eight key-image sketches. These are previs, not final art.

The selected profile is recorded once in `V35-chapter-registry.tsv` and copied into the manifest. It changes how much evidence is required, not the artifact names or gate sequence. `Light` still receives the independent first-read causality audit, semantic human-voice review, and explicit user acceptance. `Heavy` requires the physical board; `Standard` uses it when scale, simultaneous location, control ownership, or movement would otherwise remain ambiguous.

## First-Read Causality Audit

Run immediately after the complete chapter outline and before episode splitting, state ledgers, L4.5, or Reader drafting.

Before dispatching the audit, compile its predecessor input from the active chapter registry and the predecessor's current manifest. Every supplied handoff or state file must declare an active status and match the registry chapter title, predecessor, episode count, and current Reader/state-ledger hashes. Files registered as `history`, `superseded`, `retired`, or `_history` evidence are forbidden audit inputs unless the task explicitly asks for historical comparison. A stale handoff that disagrees with the current registry is a source-lifecycle defect, not evidence that the new outline must be rewritten to match it.

The reviewer receives:

- the readable outline;
- the previous chapter's concise outgoing state;
- the current chapter's one-sentence engine and promised exit state.

The audit receipt must list the exact predecessor artifacts and their statuses. If provenance cannot be established, return `INPUT_BLOCKED` before judging story causality.

The reviewer does not receive the director board, physical defense, mechanism glossary, or the lead writer's intended interpretation. It checks whether a first-time reader can answer:

- who wants what and why the conflict begins now;
- why each side acts instead of using an easier option;
- where people and machines are during decisive events;
- who controls each consequential body, weapon, system, or ability;
- what makes the victory or loss possible;
- what changed irreversibly and why the next chapter follows.

Failure returns to the earliest owning layer. The lead writer repairs the outline and reruns the audit; the reviewer does not append explanations to the story.

## Director and Battle Preplan

Before the complete outline, establish:

- scene order and point of view;
- emotional waveform and relationship actions;
- chapter spectacle images and transitions;
- player-visible evidence;
- provisional performance carrier: ADV, exploration, SRPG, map event, in-battle communication, Cut-in, short animation, settlement;
- human-readable battle goal;
- map silhouette, sides, stages, operable units, battle interruptions, and production budget.

If the player cannot explain “who wants what and what must be stopped,” the battle is not ready.

## Foreground Information Budget

Backstage may distinguish ten objects or systems. Frontstage should normally require the player to understand one decisive causal/control change in a scene.

Spread explanation across:

- location;
- repeated object;
- UI state change;
- whole-body machine action;
- character choice;
- consequence in the next scene.

Do not explain all objects in one conversation. A technically correct outline can still be blocked for Reader when its foreground load is too high.

## Fault Router

| Symptom | Return To |
| --- | --- |
| Nobody urgently wants anything | chapter engine |
| Character knows impossible information | limited-knowledge sandbox |
| AI, pilot, machine soul, remote layer, or weapon ownership is unclear | entity/control model |
| Drawing, name, model, or data directly changes reality | ability execution chain |
| Giant body, human, carrier, or facility cannot share the action | spatial graybox |
| Battle requires prose to explain tiny movements | action vocabulary and battle preplan |
| New room/device appears only to solve a scene | asset/reuse model |
| Relationship “turn” is only a conclusion | director/performance pass |
| Dialogue sounds like design documentation | semantic human-voice gate |
| Fact contradicts authority or outgoing state | Canon/status migration |

Do not fix downstream symptoms while leaving the owner layer unchanged.

## Performance Before Dialogue

For each key conversation, write a short performance brief:

- who is listening;
- what each person wants now;
- what each refuses to say;
- body distance and current task;
- who interrupts, mishears, evades, jokes, or leaves;
- the physical action that changes the scene;
- the emotional leak;
- what remains unresolved.

Then write dialogue. Dialogue carries a person speaking to a listener, not a field explanation.

## Semantic Human-Voice Gate

Run this after the Reader or script exists and before deterministic lint.

### Tests

- **Anonymous test**: can readers still identify the speaker without the name?
- **Swap test**: can two speakers exchange lines with little damage? If yes, voices are not distinct.
- **Listener test**: why does this person say this to this listener now?
- **Knowledge test**: does the speaker know this, or is the author borrowing their mouth?
- **Action test**: does the line change behavior, distance, pressure, or decision?
- **Silence test**: would an action, interruption, look, bad joke, or unfinished sentence work better?
- **Read-aloud test**: can a human actor say it without sounding like a report?

### Common Failures

- narrator pre-defends the intended interpretation;
- narrator repeatedly says what an object, person, or relationship is *not*
  instead of showing the positive carrier, action, and consequence;
- characters submit responsibility or ethics reports;
- technical cookbook wording dominates;
- symmetrical debate where everyone is equally articulate;
- everyone produces aphorisms and subtext;
- parenthetical action replaces actual blocking and performance;
- the scene ends by restating its thesis;
- adult attraction is declared by narration instead of desire, risk, embarrassment, jealousy, tenderness, or physical choice.

Natural speech does not mean every line must be short or colloquial. Characters may be formal, strange, numerical, theatrical, or machine-like when the listener, personality, and moment support it.

Reader prose may use a concentrated clarification once when an ontology boundary
is genuinely necessary. Repeated defensive frames such as “不是另一个人”,
“没有谁替谁”, “没有和好”, or “不等于” are reviewer-facing prose and fail
the gate when the same fact can be carried by visible location, memory,
control, interruption, or behavior. Put exhaustive exclusions in open
information or backstage ledgers, not in the dramatic paragraph.

A dispute also fails when all speakers deliver balanced position papers. Give
each participant a different immediate want, tolerance for silence, sentence
length, willingness to interrupt, and physical task. One may answer the wrong
question, stop speaking, leave, make a bad joke, or act before explaining.

Battle prose should foreground objective, reversal, one or two readable
whole-body actions, and the character choice that changes the map. Exact
modules, permissions, timing, and exception rules belong in gameplay/open
information unless the player must understand them in that instant.

## Deterministic Lint Boundary

Use scripts to find:

- banned or recurrent sentence frames;
- technical-word density;
- repeated thesis constructions;
- speaker-format errors;
- obvious duplication.

Zero findings do not prove natural dialogue. A semantic reviewer may block a draft that passes lint, and this is expected.

## Review Ownership

- One lead writer owns Reader prose and final integration.
- Independent reviewers diagnose symptom, evidence, root cause, earliest owner layer, and scene to change.
- Reviewers do not each append fixes to the Reader.
- Use targeted roles by risk; do not run a large writer room by default.

Before accepting any external review, run a source-fidelity triage. Every
finding must identify the current artifact, chapter/episode, and exact evidence,
then receive one label:

- `valid`;
- `valid_but_wrong_layer`;
- `already_present`;
- `stale_source`;
- `mislocated`;
- `invalid_test`.

Do not patch a current draft when the reviewer cites the wrong chapter, proposes
text that is already present, silently treats its own rewrite as source text, or
uses a character identity/control fact contradicted by the current packet.
Aesthetic observations can still be useful, but they need independent evidence.

## Artifacts and Cost

Within the preproduction step, the normal maximum is:

1. one readable preproduction model;
2. one optional machine table;
3. one review result.

Light chapters may fold all three into existing boards. Heavy chapters add only the previs needed to verify space, scale, control, and spectacle.

The same fact should have one owner. Other documents link to it instead of rewriting it.

The final gate belongs to the user. Internal chapter review may recommend
acceptance, but it cannot seal `user_acceptance`, initialize the next chapter,
or convert silence into approval. Record explicit approval with
`scripts/record_v35_user_acceptance.py`; any upstream content change
invalidates the receipt through the normal dependency chain.

This limit does not replace a project-level Story Chain whose manifest already
requires separate alignment, director, battle, physical, outline, state,
performance, Reader, and review artifacts. In such projects:

- keep the manifest's standard filenames and stage responsibilities;
- reduce a light chapter by shortening and linking, not by inventing a second
  directory shape;
- place temporary experiments and accepted side research under `support/`;
- move superseded trials and review evidence under `_history/`;
- run the project packet-consistency check before sealing a stage.

For V35 use:

```bash
python3 scripts/check_v35_chapter_packet_consistency.py \
  --chapter Cxx \
  --strict

python3 scripts/check_v35_story_chain.py \
  --manifest docs/superpowers/specs/剧情梗概/V35.9/rebuild/manifests/Cxx-story-chain.tsv \
  --before-stage reader_causal_audit
```

The packet checker validates current production only. When a chapter has no
`passed` or `generated` artifact it must fail instead of printing PASS.
`--allow-noncurrent` is permitted only for explicit inventory inspection.

Chapter Reader assembly follows manifest Episode paths and `stage_order`
exactly. It must not glob the Episode directory. The assembled Reader records
each source filename and hash so an old copy cannot enter silently.

For C02 and later, `series_alignment` stores
`predecessor_fingerprint=<sha256>` in its existing notes. The fingerprint covers
the predecessor's current chapter Reader, state ledger, and explicit user
acceptance. A changed or stale predecessor blocks the current chapter until the
alignment is invalidated and resealed.

Before `reader_causal_audit`, the story-chain gate emits the cross-chapter risk
scan and the exact receipt header. The scanner raises candidates in five
categories:

- `FIRST_SCOPE`: first/never claims whose world, chapter, or gameplay scope may
  be unclear;
- `RELATIVE_TIME`: yesterday, tonight, dawn, or similar wording that needs an
  event anchor;
- `ORPHAN_EVIDENCE`: wreckage, fragments, samples, or evidence without a
  visible origin;
- `OBJECT_RETURN`: destroyed, removed, or displaced objects that return;
- `SPACE_TRANSITION`: arrivals, departures, returns, docking, and launch
  transitions that must inherit the prior spatial anchor.

These are review candidates, not forbidden phrases. The auditor resolves or
waives every blocking candidate in prose and seals only a full-scope receipt
containing exact `Reader-SHA256`, `Prefix-SHA256`, `Risk-Scan-SHA256`,
`Result: PASS`, `Open-P0: 0`, and `Open-Blocking-P1: 0`.

The audit artifact's H1 must include the chapter id and current registry title.
Its body must state the Reader-only evidence boundary and explicitly close or
block chronology and travel, human and mecha positions, scale and loads,
control ownership, evidence provenance, fail-safe behavior and the simplest
competent alternative, and first/return/launch claims against preceding
chapters. A generic “physical audit”, an old Reader hash, or a report that
silently uses the physical board is not a valid receipt even if it says PASS.

## Regression Suite

Use C01-C05 failures as tests, not as content templates:

- assembly: an extra stale Episode file must not enter a chapter Reader;
- packet state: a chapter with no current artifact must not claim PASS;
- cross-chapter: changing predecessor Reader, state, or acceptance must stale
  the next chapter alignment;
- receipt binding: an older Reader SHA, prefix SHA, risk-scan SHA, partial
  scope, open P0, or blocking P1 must reject the audit receipt;
- risk candidates: first/never, relative time, orphan evidence, object return,
  and space transition must appear in auditable output without becoming bans;
- C01: accepted draft still contained impossible route time, missing G.A.G. signal carrier, and narrator-declared relationship meaning;
- C02: flying ability versus legal air-route identity, object overload, and narrator-resolved triangle;
- C03: unseen civilian safety, joint-scale action too small for spectacle, and settlement overload;
- C04: one physical U-shield implied in two places; Baiyuan's false route lacked distinct real executors;
- C05: legal trial permission confused with forged transport permission; transport revocation confused with remote control; disconnect danger only explained; a worker platform appeared at crisis time; Baixiang left without a complete loading chain; deterministic dialogue lint missed semantic AI voice.
- C05 operator route: leaving a remote pod and later becoming passengers in
  that pod requires a visible return, seat lock, and bridge retraction before
  the accident can use them as stakes.
- C05 command chain: changing disconnect braking to hold-last-action requires a
  named signer, visible push station, target ownership, exceeded safety
  boundary, and operator objection.
- C05 movement split: external ground handling and onboard short-slide
  propulsion require distinct names, controls, and responsible actors.
- C05 movement source: a large carrier cannot change position merely because
  the outline says it entered a frame; show the surviving ground tractor or
  onboard propulsion that supplies the force.
- C05 available-tool conservation: if the surviving ground tractor remains
  powered and connected after towing the carrier into the frame, the later
  onboard short-slide is unjustified. The story must visibly depower or
  disconnect the tractor and establish why it cannot be reattached in time.
- C05 detachable pod: carriage by belly hoist, seat return/restraint, lack of
  independent flight, attitude-only jets, and recovery interface must all be
  visible before pod separation can become a climax.
- C05 pressure boundary: taking the server must open an exterior unpressurized
  equipment cover while leaving the fixed pressure bulkhead intact; a later
  launch cannot follow an unexplained pressure-hull breach.
- C05 rescue closure: Baixiang catching a falling pod does not pass unless the
  combined mass is arrested and returned by a visible support chain such as
  Guardian Z's tow anchor and Wanji's main winch.
- Cross-chapter forecast conservation: a promised destination, support arrival,
  or future handoff must match the later physical route and arrival scene.
- Late naming regression: after anonymous operators receive names, rescan
  collective pronouns and seat labels. If their survival carries emotional
  weight, use at least one name again in the conflict or payoff.
- Continuous Reader rhythm: keep disclosure cards outside pure-story episode
  sources and chapter assembly; link them only from the production bundle or
  optional in-game archive.
- C05 semantic voice: a Reader that explains itself through repeated defensive
  negations, balanced position papers, or narrator-declared relationship state
  fails even when deterministic phrase lint is clean.

Passing the suite means the workflow detects and routes these defects. It does not require future chapters to resemble C01-C05.

## Pass Levels

- `PASS`: no open root-cause blocker; warnings belong to a named downstream layer.
- `PASS_WITH_ADVISORY`: the current layer can advance, but a later performance, asset, or Reader gate remains.
- `BLOCK_FOR_READER`: design can advance, prose cannot.
- `BLOCK`: return to the earliest owning layer.
- `INVALID_TEST`: wrong authority, stale object, lint-only claim, or missing counterexample.
