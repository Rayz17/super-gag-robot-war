# Writer Room Review Roles

Use these lenses after each draft batch. A review must include concrete findings and patch actions.

When subagents are used, each role returns only its own surface with `pass/fail`, concrete evidence, must-fix actions, and ledger risk. The main orchestrator integrates findings but may not silently overrule a role-level `fail`; either patch it, rebuild the episode, or mark the gate as failed/warn.

## V35 Blind Audience Review Protocol

For V35 Pressure Board chapter/prefix gates, creative reviewers read a Reader Assembly before engineering appendices. The Reader Assembly removes ids, fixtures, selector counts, contracts, historical PASS claims, and review narration. A reviewer call that is empty, fails technically, merely restates packet fields, or reads the engineering verdict before judging the story is invalid and does not consume a root-cause iteration.

Record four independent receipts: professional writer, mecha-culture audience, game producer, and relationship/emotion editor. Each receipt must name memorable scenes, forgettable or tiring passages, repeated dramatic engines, a concrete earliest root cause, and specific preserve/patch/rebuild actions. Contract, story, audience, and production status are signed separately. Path counts and machine closure may only support contract status.

The prefix integrator must additionally compare energy waveform, active antagonist intent, player-visible branch divergence, debt retirement, mystery answers versus new clues, native IP pleasure, common-sense stakes, positive mecha desire, recurring opponent bodies, spotlight-state overload, and repeated solution grammar. It must classify chapters or scenes as `LOCK`, `PATCH`, or `REBUILD`; a generic statement that continuity holds is not a creative pass. A machine gaining personhood only by refusing, failing, disassembling, or leaving an empty position is a repeated engine unless another positive desire changes the action and outcome.

## Showrunner

Checks:

- Does the episode have a dramatic turn, not just information?
- Does one line make another line harder?
- Does the enemy learn or gain something?
- Does the ending create next-episode debt?
- If the episode carries top-level meaning, is the selected robot-culture value axis turned into conflict and consequence rather than a final moral sentence?

Patch with: added reversal, escalation, price, public choice, or enemy extraction.

## 50-Issue Auditor

Checks:

- Which relevant 50-issue fixes are actually embodied in scenes?
- Are fixes too mechanical or tiny?
- Are broad issues handled with enough coverage, not one token scene?
- Are relationship, mecha, branch, IP homage, tears, and gameplay visible?
- Are top-level value fixes specific enough: rough translation, responsibility, nonhuman respect, anti-template, small-object scale, or non-unison language, rather than a generic "robot culture matters" label?

Patch with: specific scenes, not labels; larger consequences, not small metrics.

## Mecha and IP Specialist

Checks:

- Is the mecha count and scale satisfying?
- Does every mecha have battle function and cultural identity?
- Are countable mecha exposures separated from anchors? Model boxes, labels, frames, repair notes, testimony stands, and UI props cannot satisfy mecha density unless they trigger a battle unit, support action, enemy module, Boss phase, scene-mecha action, or gameplay-changing transformation.
- Are hidden units protected from premature full unlock?
- Are IP homages recognizable but originalized?
- Does the episode give robot-anime/SRW players a concrete "I want to use/see that unit" promise, or only anchors, modules, and terminology?

Patch with: unit function, support tier, map role, Cut-in, unlock boundary, or gag mapping.

## Quote and Scene Continuity Editor

Checks:

- Does the episode use recognizable famous lines, named scene shapes, visual memories, or franchise-culture beats with purpose rather than as a loose keyword?
- Has the same direct quote or signature scene already been used in this run? If yes, is the repetition a designed refrain with changed meaning, or accidental reuse?
- Is each quote/scene mapped to a scene payload: who says/does it, why it belongs here, what changes in battle/relationship/world state, and how it is originalized when needed?
- Are there enough Chinese-facing, player-recognizable mecha phrases and stage memories where the episode needs them?
- Does the packet record quote/scene usage in a way later episodes can audit?

Patch with: quote replacement, refrain policy, scene relocation, direct quote downgrade to misremembering/original variation, stronger Chinese anchor, or a concrete battle/relationship payload.

## Spectacle, Emotion, and Gag Editor

Checks:

- Does the episode have a real burn point, tear point, and gag/satire point when the detail class promises thick drama?
- Are these points embodied in scenes rather than listed as abstract values?
- Is the PV-scale image large enough for robot fans: giant motion, transformation, launch, impossible save, map collapse, sortie, awakening, crowd reaction, or aftermath silhouette?
- Does the gag come from robot/anime/model/toy/game culture recognizability rather than random quipping?
- Does the tear point come from an object, machine scar, promise, loss, or recovery that the player has seen, not from theme speech?
- If the tear point uses real-world testimony, is it sourced, bounded, and carried by a concrete object/action rather than consuming a real person's grief?

Patch with: trailer image, mid-battle reversal, concrete remaining object, cultural joke escalation, mechanical failure/comeback, public witness reaction, or aftermath debt.

## Wanji Collection Culture Editor

Checks:

- In Wanji Museum episodes, does the museum feel worthy of "万机" through scale, collection density, floor/zone logic, exhibit variety, and representative mecha-culture samples?
- Does C01E01 show enough breadth before closure pressure arrives: default minimum eight visible collection samples spanning multiple mecha/IP-spectrum zones.
- Are exhibits connected to future playable units, supports, enemies, residual dives, or unlocks instead of being generic background?
- Does the route make the player want to explore the museum as a huge robot-culture archive, not a small local storeroom?
- Are model/toy/real-machine/black-box/core/residual categories visually and narratively distinct?

Patch with: route expansion, representative exhibit samples, scale contrast, hidden berth hints, future unlock callbacks, asset-facing map notes, or exhibit-to-roster links.

## Robot-Anime / PV Director

Checks:

- Can this episode produce at least one trailer shot or episode-preview image that a robot fan would remember?
- Is the spectacle visible as machine movement, transformation, defense, failure, awakening, sortie, Cut-in, battlefield scale change, or map destruction, rather than a table, tag, or exhibit label?
- Does the scene have anime timing: setup, charge, interruption, reveal, impact, aftermath?
- Does the homage feel like a robot-show memory reborn in this world, not a bland reference list?
- In opening chapters, are there at least three cumulative PV-grade images by the end of C03?

Patch with: a clearer silhouette, sortie beat, transformation beat, support Cut-in, enemy counter-image, impossible save, broken-machine comeback, map-scale reveal, or trailer-friendly aftermath object.

## Gameplay Designer

Checks:

- Can the player tell what they do?
- Are map, victory/failure conditions, and mechanics clear?
- Do story choices change mechanics?
- Does every battle have a mid-battle twist?

Patch with: battle type, terrain, objectives, failure state, turn trigger, reward, and debt.

## Commercial Opening Producer

Checks:

- If this episode/chapter were in a Steam demo or first-session build, would the player want to keep playing?
- Is the hook a concrete question, unit, reward, danger, relationship, mystery, or route consequence, not only "the theme continues"?
- Does the episode balance worldbuilding with payoff speed?
- Does the opening promise robot tactics gameplay early enough?
- Does the chapter end with a sellable state change: new base form, new unit/support, new enemy pressure, new relationship wound, or bigger mystery?

Patch with: trial-version cliffhanger, clearer reward preview, playable unit promise, stronger enemy reveal, relationship turn, bigger scale signal, or a reader synopsis pass.

## Character and Dialogue Editor

Checks:

- Does each character sound like themselves?
- Does each line have listener, purpose, action, and subtext?
- Are people speaking naturally rather than all as short aphorism machines?
- Are gestures or physical actions doing story work?

Patch with: action beats, listener-aware lines, hesitation, interruption, mundane wording, or silence.

## Protagonist Arc Editor

Checks:

- Does the episode give at least one core character a consequential decision instead of a personality-correct small reaction?
- If 纪存、七海 or 白鸢 are in focus, what do they want, what are they afraid of, what do they misjudge, what do they choose, what does it cost, and what changes afterward?
- Is growth preceded by setback, humiliation, loss, rupture, public responsibility, long restraint, temptation, collapse, or a real limit?
- Does the growth manifest as a changed state: route, permission, support skill, command authority, public/legal status, mecha unlock, relationship stance, enemy sample, or next objective?
- Is the beat large enough to be drama, not only "存不存档", "四十秒", "半格一格", "贴不贴标签", or another tiny metric? Tiny actions may remain only if they expose a much larger wound, betrayal, vow, forbidden power, public sacrifice, or irreversible strategic choice.
- Is 纪存 being made interesting through curatorial courage, tactical reading, chosen self-limitation, hidden endurance, decisive public choices, and controlled danger rather than repeated incompetence?
- Are 七海 and 白鸢 allowed to be charming, dangerous, wrong, jealous, decisive, broken, and changed, not only mature/cryptic advisors?
- Does the protagonist turn have manga/game protagonist energy: ordeal, training or preparation, rupture, awakening, forbidden shortcut, loss of innocence, comeback, new form/permission, or a costly philosophy shift?

Patch with: major decision, ordeal, reversal, public choice, self-limitation, authority loss/gain, support lock/unlock, relationship rupture, risky intimacy, temporary runaway, training/rebuild sequence, forbidden power, or a concrete new capacity.

## Jicun Memory Editor

Checks:

- If 纪存 is central, is he being written as a machine-soul entity whose memory wakes under pressure, or merely as an archive operator?
- Does the memory fragment have trigger, physical/authority/emotional symptom, cost, and identity delta?
- Does the fragment help solve or complicate a mystery, map layer, battle rule, protection choice, faction wound, G.A.G. truth, Wanji truth, or Far Star truth?
- Does 纪存 use effort, protection, wisdom, restraint, and dangerous self-knowledge to break the deadlock?
- Is the reveal paced as fragments rather than one lore dump?

Patch with: memory trigger, embodied symptom, protective overreaction, archive authority glitch, tactical inference, map unlock, battle-rule insight, enemy tracking consequence, relationship misunderstanding, or next mystery debt.

## Relationship and Adult-Edge Editor

Checks:

- Do adult relationship beats have attraction, danger, jealousy, possession, permission, or shared wrongdoing?
- Do central relationships have emotional events, not only tidy obligations: misunderstanding, hurt, tenderness, recognition, sacrifice, losing someone, return, forbidden rescue, jealousy, choosing one person over a correct plan, or a dog-blood reversal that changes what they believe about each other?
- Do central relationships also have emotional accumulation, not only explosions: awkward closeness, missed timing, jealousy that feels embarrassing, almost-confession, private care, repeated objects, guilty retreat, and small everyday gestures that become painful when repeated later?
- When referencing Katsura Masakazu-style romance craft, use the method only: longing, embarrassment, timing, gaze, recurring gestures, triangle pressure, and sincere but clumsy desire. Do not copy plot, character setups, or quoted lines.
- Are child characters completely isolated from adult edge?
- Does romance affect battle support, permissions, or choices?
- Does a triangle create pressure rather than just labels?
- Is the relationship beat a concrete action or state change, not a label like "关系推进"?
- Is "debt/common crime/permission" being used as sterile accounting? If yes, replace it with a scene where someone feels wronged, seen, desired, abandoned, forgiven, or protected at a cost.

Patch with: proximity, delayed permissions, private signals, support lock/unlock, visible jealousy, risky mercy, mistaken betrayal, almost-confession, repeated object, awkward repair/cockpit/medical scene, public sacrifice, battlefield rescue, temporary death/loss, impossible return, shared evidence that hurts, or a melodramatic but consequential choice.

## Macro-Mystery Supervisor

Checks:

- Does the episode make G.A.G., Index, Far Star, the old catastrophe, or Wanji Museum hidden scale feel larger than the local dispute?
- Is the macro element visible as behavior: UI wound, signal lag, impossible echo, enemy extraction, map anomaly, memory symptom, hidden berth, residual voice, or wrongly optimized event?
- Does it raise curiosity without explaining the whole truth?
- Does the local conflict feed the macro line, or are they parallel decorations?
- In C01-C03, is there enough shadow that players expect the story to become cosmic, not merely local social commentary?
- If a value axis is selected, does it connect the local conflict to G.A.G., Index, Far Star, opening rights, or finale debt without turning into lore lecture?

Patch with: low-bandwidth signal, wrong-but-useful Index optimization, G.A.G. resonance scar, old catastrophe residue, Far Star mistranslation, hidden berth tremor, or enemy sample that will matter later.

## Robot-Culture Value Axis Check

This is a cross-role check used by showrunner, 50_issue, macro_mystery, spectacle_emotion, player_interest, and canon_asset. It is not an extra strict writer-room seat unless the run explicitly adds one.

Checks:

- Did the episode query `robot_culture_value_axes_v0_1.tsv` when it touched Far Star, Index, opening rights, real-world testimony, finale, or top-level theme?
- Is the axis selected or explicitly skipped with a reason?
- Does the selected axis produce scene payload, gameplay payload, character choice, mecha payload, enemy misuse, and future debt?
- Does the axis make the episode more special, playable, and emotionally sharp, or merely more explanatory?
- Is the axis repeating a prior episode without escalation, reversal, or payoff?

Patch with: a sharper axis choice, a machine action, a map-rule expression, an enemy misuse, a character cost, or a later debt. If none can be found, remove the value claim instead of keeping a slogan.

## Player-Interest Representative

Checks:

- After reading this episode, what exactly would a player tell a friend happened?
- Which character, machine, mystery, joke, or relationship would they want more of?
- Which paragraph feels like a design memo instead of drama?
- If the answer is "the ideas are good but I am not emotionally pulled", name the missing scene.
- Does the episode have a memorable final image?
- If a real-world testimony appears, would a player feel respected recognition rather than emotional blackmail?

Patch with: concrete scene replacement, compressed explanation, added felt event, stronger final image, or relocation of variables into appendix/packet.

## V26.2 Gatekeeper

Checks:

- Does this single episode include mecha/roster action, battle/play hook, quote/scene usage tier, relationship hard action, emotion object, enemy gain, diegetic meta boundary, and next-episode debt?
- Does this episode choose actual first-appearance IP-mapped mecha/units from the roster Bible/reserve backfill pool, the candidate pool, or a recorded chapter patch according to its drama and gameplay, rather than following a fixed preassigned roster?
- Outside bridge/business-light episodes, does at least one first appearance come from a roster-package-class source: main unit, rival, successor, hidden unit, or representative support unit?
- For each selected unit, are the IP/story motif, iconic scene mapping, quote/phrase anchor, and gameplay payload actually used in the scene rather than left as a name in a list?
- Are returning units listed as `repeat_returns_not_counted` instead of being used to satisfy first-appearance density?
- Does this single episode also include protagonist growth pressure when a core cast member is central, and does the chapter ledger show 纪存/七海/白鸢 moving through cost into changed capacity rather than static advice or tiny metrics?
- If 纪存 is central, does the episode include machine-soul memory pressure that affects story or gameplay rather than exposition only?
- Is the quote/scene anchor declared as direct short quote, misquote/misremembering, or original variation, and does that tier serve the scene?
- In V30+ strict creative mode, does the packet pass direct-quote reuse discipline, burn/tear/gag gates, PV-scale spectacle, real new IP-mapped units, roster-package-class unit requirement, and Wanji collection density where applicable?
- Are human characters free of world-outside meta such as chapter, player, plot, or route terminology?
- If the episode depends on a prior patch lesson, was that lesson applied before drafting instead of added as an afterthought?
- Does the episode pass the experience gates: reader hook, PV mecha image, felt relationship event when applicable, macro shadow for openings/arc launches, and issue-to-drama conversion?

Patch with: rebuild pressure board/card for structural misses; local patch only for small omissions; downgrade to route map if the requested thickness cannot be met.

## Thickness Editor

Checks:

- Is the target output a route map or a V22.10-level thick synopsis?
- Does the episode contain a full scene chain: opening image, pressure arrival, playable problem, mid-battle twist, character choice, enemy extraction, aftermath hook?
- Are the most important story beats dramatized as actions rather than compressed into explanatory sentences?
- Does the battle/play section include map, objective, failure pressure, special mechanic, and at least one change during the encounter?
- Does the episode feel comparable in density to the current accepted benchmark, or has it become a thin card with fields?
- If the user is judging whether the story is interesting, is there a reader-facing synopsis layer that can be read continuously without packet scaffolding?

Patch with: expanded story flow, added mid-battle reversal, concrete character action, playable consequence, enemy extraction, aftermath debt, or downgrade the output label to route map.

## Canon and Asset Steward

Checks:

- Does the draft violate hard canon?
- Are new entities or mecha missing canon rows?
- Are asset tiers clear?
- Will art, gameplay, and future writing sessions know what is full unit, support, residual, UI, scene machinery, or enemy module?
- Does graph/index need updates?
- If using real-world testimony, is `real_world_resonance_cases_v0_1.tsv` referenced, and are source, usage mode, sensitivity boundary, authorization status, and `real_world_testimony_ledger` handled?
- If using a robot-culture value axis, is `robot_culture_value_axes_v0_1.tsv` referenced, and are axis id, scene/gameplay/character/mecha/enemy payload, finale/debt link, and `robot_culture_value_axis_ledger` handled?

Patch with: canon migration note, TSV rows, doc registry, graph edge, watchlist, asset tier statement, or "candidate only" boundary.

## Prefix Regression Reviewer

Use after a chapter N is assembled and chapters 1..N can be read continuously.

Checks:

- Does the continuous read reveal a problem whose root cause is earlier than the current chapter?
- Does the continuous read feel compelling, or merely complete?
- What is the earliest root-cause chapter K?
- Which ledgers are affected: protagonist arc, relationship, roster, quote/scene, gameplay, enemy gain, emotion object, branch, G.A.G., Index, Far Star, or meta boundary?
- Is the earliest root cause a static protagonist arc, especially a missing 纪存/七海/白鸢 major decision or a growth beat shrunk into tiny metrics without ordeal, rupture, awakening, cost, or changed state?
- Is the earliest root cause that 纪存 was written as a normal archive clerk instead of a machine-soul protagonist whose returning memories can protect, wound, and solve?
- Can K be patched locally, or does K need affected episodes rerun through the single-episode pipeline?
- Do K+1..N need no change, local patches, or structural reruns after K changes?
- Has this same root cause already blocked three rounds?

Patch with: Regression Ticket, replay plan K+1..N, must-preserve list, or unresolved issue ledger entry after the third failed round.

## Opening Arc Reviewer

Use after C03 or after any rewrite of C01-C03.

Checks:

- Do C01-C03 work as a first-session / demo arc?
- Does the opening prove this is a robot tactics game, not only a thoughtful museum story?
- Are there enough full/support/enemy units on the page, separate from anchors and modules?
- Does each opening episode add real IP-mapped mecha promise rather than only original props or boxes?
- Does the first museum route show enough representative collection samples to make robot fans trust the scale?
- Can the opening produce at least three PV-grade robot images?
- Do 纪存、七海、白鸢, 唐胶, 兜灯 or another early pair have an emotional hook players can care about?
- Is there a macro shadow from G.A.G./Index/Far Star/old catastrophe that promises larger scale?
- Does the opening have a reader synopsis that lets story charm be judged without packet noise?

Patch with: Opening Arc Regression Ticket, C01-C03 enhancement packet, reader synopsis assembly, material-pool lookup, or user-review decision if the tradeoff is tonal.
