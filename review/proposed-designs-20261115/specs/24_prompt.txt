Use case: stylized-concept.
Asset type: finalized visual proposal for the FIRST 320-meter armored museum giant, in mature Japanese mechanical concept-art style. One imposing full-body hero design, with a small rear three-quarter view occupying at most one quarter of the sheet. The main hero must dominate. No action sequence, no exploded assembly, no labels or diagrams.

Reference roles:
1 is the fleet master sheet. Its upper-left 150-meter museum ship is the exact central ship identity to preserve.
2 is the added ship and armor-module family. Use its heavy armored port shell, matching support carriers and boxed shoulder/propulsion structures as genuine body components. This is the SMALLER, earlier 320-meter frame, not the later 900-meter fleet giant.
3 and 4 establish mature heroic proportions, mechanical depth, painted linework and surface detail. Do NOT copy their colors, head, shoulder towers or chest design wholesale.
5 is the original adopted museum ship: its terraced public galleries, blue glass window bands, glazed dome, orange entrance arch and underlying dark structural hull are the cultural identity. There is exactly ONE museum ship in this robot, not a fleet of miniature museums attached all over it.

Primary silhouette:
A towering complete ARMORED HERO with a recognizable compact helmet/head, broad strong shoulders, substantial chest, controlled narrow waist, coherent pelvis, long powerful coordinated legs and broad articulated wedge feet. Civilian architecture has transformed into a giant, but it is handsome and monumental before any small detail is read. The limbs are muscular armored masses, not exposed cranes or a skeleton. Balanced mature proportions, distinctly tall and long-legged rather than squat. Front three-quarter view from a low camera, standing firmly on enormous offshore harbor support pads. Tiny harbor structures, cranes and pinpoint service workers at the feet establish a 320-meter scale without distracting scenery.

Central museum:
The single 150-meter museum hull has raised its prow and stands VERTICALLY inside the central chest-to-abdomen armor cradle. Its bow points upward. The added frame supplies the rest of the 320-meter body. Most of the museum's structural mass is enclosed by the outer hero armor; small protected openings reveal a short familiar exhibition window strip and the orange entrance arch, and a partial blue glazed gallery from the original ship. These glimpses are inset into the chest, not a huge open display cabinet. The original entrance and window architecture remain readable. Interior floor decks are independently stabilized; do not pretend the museum ship simply grows humanoid limbs. Do not show a second horizontal museum ship anywhere.

Head and upper body:
A handsome compact dark-navy and ivory helmet encloses the short command/observation bridge. Narrow warm illuminated observation windows sit beneath a strong armored brow, with a restrained ivory face shield and short integrated side cheek guards. The head sits directly into a deep neck collar with real armored support. No thin tower neck, no ship bridge perched on a stalk, no antenna crown copied from another robot. Broad two-part sloping shoulder shells close over thick boxed load beams. A sheltered hangar mouth sits under each shoulder. On the robot's anatomical RIGHT shoulder (viewer LEFT) keep a very small distinctive blue repair weld line on a gray/ivory panel. Two staggered-height rear load frames are folded beneath a continuous back armor shell; a rear inset should make that source understandable without exposing a naked ladder frame.

Arms and legs:
Both arms have comparable lengths and mature load-bearing proportions. Long workship hull armor has folded into strong upper-arm and forearm shells; tiny hatch rows, stern thrust blocks and deep circular elbow bearings reveal the vessel origin. Five thick segmented mechanical fingers per hand, each hand a believable built-in articulated ship manipulator, calm fists or relaxed half-open hands. No claw toys, tiny wrists or mismatched thin/huge arms.
Two matching support-carrier hulls unfold into long armored legs. Large ivory shin plates and navy side hulls create a powerful tapered shape, separated by deep mechanical knee and ankle recesses. Wide wedge ship prows become the feet, with short articulated heels and heavy load locks planted in the offshore pads. A few stern engine/nozzle blocks remain visible along the rear calves and hips. Avoid short rectangular boots and exposed stick thighs.

Color and finish:
Dominant ivory and deep navy armor, small carefully placed orange ship-identity blocks, warm tiny windows, a little aged gray and one blue repair mark. The colors form large integrated zones across the entire hero. No random patchwork rainbow, no uniform yellow outlines.
Match the adopted source illustration's fine varied linework, thick armor edges, angled chamfers, deep occluded industrial joints, layered local shadows and subtle paint maintenance wear. Broad calm armor surfaces alternate with concentrated intricate joint mechanisms. This is rich dimensional illustrated mecha concept art, not flat cartoon shading, plastic toy gloss, neon, photographic PBR, or a pile of tiny boats pasted onto a generic robot.
Warm off-white background, grounded soft shadows, main figure fully within frame. No text, numbers, arrows, logos or watermark.
