Use case: stylized-concept.
Asset type: a mature Japanese science fiction mechanical design master sheet, FOUR independent spacecraft, large detailed landscape illustration. This is the single-craft design stage for a later monumental transforming fleet robot. Draw only the independent ships now, not any combined robot.

Input references and roles:
1 is the adopted museum ship. Preserve that ship's recognizable physical identity: its terraced ivory public decks, long blue window bands, glazed dome, orange entrance arch, broad forked bow promenade, lower navy structural hull, separate orange-capped engines, and short aft bridge.
2, 3 and 4 define the mature spacecraft design language: long coherent hulls, large angular armor planes, wedge bows, recessed hangars, functional thick skins and deep machinery. Do not copy their alternative designs or letters in the margins.
5 defines PAINTING DEPTH AND MECHANICAL VOLUME, not robot shapes to insert: powerful large planes, bevel thickness, layered dark occlusion between armor, selective exposed mechanical anatomy, subtle maintained paint wear, fine variable-weight drawing, convincing scale.

Layout: spacious two-by-two arrangement. Each entire ship isolated with generous cream background and no overlapping contours. All seen from the same elevated three-quarter front viewpoint, long hull axes diagonal across the sheet. Ships are shown at comparable page size for readability, not asserted to be the same real size. Tiny human/service-vehicle or shuttle scale cues may accompany each. No captions, names, numbers, logos, watermark, arrows or decorative diagram frames. Fill the sheet with detailed handsome ship portraits rather than making a grid of tiny icons.

Top left: the 150-meter museum cruise ship from reference 1, carefully retained, with its public architectural terraces, entrance arch and glass dome intact. The lower hull has real depth and substantial engines. The ship should still feel like a beloved mobile cultural building and a credible spacecraft, elegant and imposing rather than toy-like.

Top right: an independently propelled BRIDGE-RESCUE SHIP. A long, wide, armored bridge-ship with a heavy navy and ivory wedge prow and subtly blue-green identification panels. Large slanted flank plates enclose its massive load structure; bridge windows are narrow, human-scaled. Two deep protected buffered tow-drum recesses, stout docking collars along its flanks, and broad internal load decks establish its rescue role. Include a retracted armored bridging deck, not a naked crane scaffold. Its bow, stern engines and long hull must read as one complete ship, not a little tug. This craft will later support a damaged giant's left shoulder, but do not depict it connected now.

Bottom left: an independent MAINTENANCE CRUISER. A longer industrial ship with ivory and charcoal armor, a few muted ochre panels, and a compact asymmetrical bridge. A telescoping segmented coolant/service arm is nested along the lower-left side of its hull, with an articulated tool head, hose reel, protected maintenance galleries and worker access recesses. Large shell panels cover most of the chassis; intricate mechanisms are concentrated where they work. The arm can retract into the hull and the ship can leave independently. Make it handsome, heavy and coherent, not a floating toolbox.

Bottom right: a fast ESCORT GUNSHIP with broad folded wing-shields. Dark charcoal/navy with ivory armor and muted dark crimson panels, long sharp prow and muscular narrow waist, rear vector engines, integrated naval weapon batteries. Folded shield wings sweep along the hull as substantial angled armor, not solar panels. One isolated damaged side docking socket is visibly capped/under repair; it is NOT a load-bearing partner. It provides outboard covering fire and protects small evacuation craft. Keep the whole ship elongated and formidable; do not make a stubby service boat with a cannon pasted on it.

Art finish: closely match the sophisticated illustrated source art. Refined dark linework, large restrained color regions, dimensional chamfered plating, strongly recessed joints, layered local shading and restrained metallic edge highlights. Fine hatches, window rows and occasional paint wear provide scale without covering every surface. The primary silhouette must be striking and readable before the detail. No plastic toy gloss, no chibi rounding, no thick uniform cartoon outlines, no flat infographic simplification, no neon, no photorealistic PBR. Calm warm off-white studio background.
